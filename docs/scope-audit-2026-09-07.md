# Scope Audit: 2026-09-07

## Audit Basis

This audit covers the **29 uncommitted candidate additions** relative to commit `e306061`: the latest batch of 21 and the preceding batch of 8. It is not a new audit of the 100 already committed main-list papers.

The decision rule is the repository's existing [five inclusion criteria](../README.md#strict-inclusion-criteria), together with the [contribution review requirements](../CONTRIBUTING.md#before-opening-a-pull-request): a directly medical or biomedical task, a system state, executable state transitions, operational prediction/simulation/planning/control, and implemented evaluation. Titles and the words "world model" or "digital twin" are not evidence by themselves.

The written criteria allow dynamics that are **learned or computed**. Consequently, passing this catalog's operational scope is not equivalent to being a learned world model in the narrower machine-learning sense. Mechanistic simulation, learning a transition operator, and learning parameters of a physiological model are distinguished below. Fitting parameters does not by itself make a mechanistic simulator a learned latent world model.

**Result: 27 retained additions; main-list count 129 -> 127.** The two held papers are not classified as out of scope. Their abstracts describe relevant dynamic simulations, but a methods-level inclusion decision remains pending. The 21-paper latest batch remains intact; 6 of the preceding 8 remain in the main list.

No retained candidate was found to be only static risk prediction, static cohort synthesis, geometry reconstruction, a conceptual architecture, or a benchmark without an implemented dynamical model. This is a scope decision based on the reported methods and experiments, not a reproduction of results or an endorsement of clinical efficacy.

| SBINN patient-specific artificial pancreas | Neural parameter inference personalizes a glucose-insulin ODE; numerical integration propagates physiological states under insulin actions. | Executes offline-RL insulin policies in personalized physiological simulators. | [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smhl.2026.100633), framework, parameter inference and offline-RL results. The SBINN paper's framework and surrogate-environment descriptions refer to **two representative patients** for policy learning. Its results discuss **six patients**; the Fig. 6 caption says five while listing six patient panels. Parameter inference is reported for six patients. The audit therefore does **not** change the entry to claim "policy testing only in two patients," which would contradict the results section. The qualification note instead flags the inconsistency and describes the demonstrated simulation mechanism without an unqualified policy-cohort count.

## Catalog Changes

- Retained the 27 candidates with primary methods-level support under the current scope.
- Held the two abstract-only candidates outside the main and topic lists without labeling them out of scope.
- Restricted Surgical WAM to demonstrated control capability instead of a separately validated forecasting capability.
- Flagged the SBINN paper's inconsistent policy-experiment counts without selecting one contradictory denominator as authoritative.
- Added the accessible OsteoOpt++ preprint in all three catalog locations and distinguished dynamic chewing simulation from longitudinal bone-healing prediction.
- Made the VT uncertainty model's mechanistic transition explicit.

Clinical validation, causal identification, reproducibility, and scope are separate questions. In particular, a medical task evaluated only in a phantom or simulator is still medically grounded, but its results must not be presented as demonstrated clinical benefit.
